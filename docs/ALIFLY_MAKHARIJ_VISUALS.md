# ALIFLY — Arabic articulation visual reference

## Visual model

The letter lesson uses the traditional Arabic articulation model: 17 specific makhārij grouped into five broad regions (oral cavity, throat, tongue, lips and nasal passage). The interactive side view highlights the active point; the lateral articulation of ض also gets a small top-view inset because a sagittal side view alone cannot show contact with the upper molars clearly.

The illustration is an original, simplified vector teaching diagram. It is not a medical scan, a photorealistic anatomy model, or a substitute for listening to a qualified teacher. Its animation is a short looping SVG/CSS motion cue (rest → contact → release), not a recorded MP4 video.

## Mapping used in the 28-letter lesson

| Articulation point | Letters | What the learner should see |
|---|---|---|
| Al-jawf — open mouth/throat cavity | ا (long alif) | Open passage; no tongue-to-teeth contact |
| Deepest throat | ه | Deep throat / glottal airflow |
| Middle throat | ع، ح | Constriction in the middle pharynx |
| Upper throat | غ، خ | Constriction nearer the mouth |
| Deepest tongue root against soft palate | ق | Back of tongue raised far back |
| Tongue root slightly forward of ق | ك | Back of tongue raised slightly forward |
| Middle of tongue against hard palate | ج، ش، ي (consonant) | Middle of tongue raised |
| Side of tongue against upper molars | ض | Side contact; side-view plus top-view inset |
| Tongue edge near upper molars/gums | ل | Tongue edge contact |
| Tongue tip against upper gum | ن | Tongue tip contact; nasal resonance noted separately |
| Tongue tip against palate behind upper incisors | ر | Tongue tip approaches the ridge behind the teeth |
| Tongue tip against roots of upper incisors | ت، د، ط | Tongue-tip contact and release |
| Tongue tip near edges of upper incisors | س، ز، ص | Narrow airflow channel |
| Tongue tip at edges of upper incisors | ث، ذ، ظ | Tongue tip at/near the teeth edge |
| Inside lower lip against upper incisors | ف | Lower lip to upper teeth |
| Both lips | ب، م، و (consonant) | Lip closure for ب/م; rounding for و |

**Important distinctions shown in the lesson:**
- ا is represented here as the long vowel (madd) and not as a glottal stop. Hamza (ء) is a separate glottal consonant.
- و and ي are shown in their consonant positions. When they function as long vowels, the sound is extended through the open oral cavity.
- The nasal passage is not the primary mouth articulation point for ن or م. It supplies nasal resonance (ghunnah) in the relevant contexts; the visual adds a secondary nasal marker for these letters.
- For emphatic ص، ض، ط، ظ, the learner is reminded that the back of the tongue is also raised.
- The side-view drawing cannot show lateral tongue-to-molar contact by itself, so ض includes a small top-view inset.

## Sources consulted

1. Islamic Manual, *The articulation points of the letters (makharij)* (17-point descriptions and letter groupings): https://islamicmanual.com/guide/prayer/reciting-well/articulation-points-makharij
2. Tilawa, *Makharij al-Huruf: the 17 Points of Articulation* (interactive grouping into five areas): https://tilawa.ai/en/tajweed-rules/group/makharij/
3. American Speech-Language-Hearing Association, *Arabic Phonemic Inventory* (Modern Standard Arabic consonant inventory and note on dialectal variation): https://www.asha.org/siteassets/uploadedfiles/multicultural/arabicphonemicinventory.pdf
4. Ouni et al., *Arabic visemes / place of articulation* (phonetic classification and distinction of emphatic consonants): https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2007/ouni07_interspeech.pdf

The traditional tajweed makhraj model and modern phonetic descriptions are related but not identical classification systems. The lesson therefore labels this as an educational articulation guide, not a universal dialect-specific phonetic transcription. In particular, teachers should review local teaching conventions for ج and the emphatic consonants before the site claims teacher approval.
